### Finding C1: Driver vs meta-observation distinction

This is the cold-reader finding I most want to highlight. Cycle hand-off discipline distinguishes:

- **Drivers:** reasons that *explain the choice made*. They predate and motivate the decision.
- **Meta-observations:** insights *surfaced by the choice*. They emerge from the decision and inform future cycles.

Cycle-76 _notes conflated these by listing the cycle-75 hand-off omission as a "consideration that drove the deviation" — but the omission didn't *change what cycle 76 could do* (audit-engagement was always authorized). It's a meta-observation: "future cycle hand-offs should systematically include all ITERATION-UNTIL-APPROVAL actions as candidate options."

Both belong in cycle _notes, but they should be labeled distinctly. Conflating them inflates honesty. Per cycle-73/74 _notes discipline (preserve as record-of-time, do not edit), cycle-76 _notes is not modified; the finding is captured in cycle-77 _notes for v2 design-input on cycle hand-off framing discipline.

This also surfaced a related honesty test: **did the orchestrator avoid Symphony because audit-engagement is genuinely higher-leverage, or because it's lower-effort?** Self-assessed effort delta is real but small (~20-30 minutes); the choice was honestly leverage-driven with effort as a tertiary consideration. Cycle-76 _notes named the leverage drivers explicitly but didn't name effort-asymmetry. Incomplete-but-honest accounting, not rationalization.
